Cash Register vs POS App for a Small Shop: What You Actually Need

When you open a small shop, stall or cafe, someone will tell you to get "a proper POS system". Someone else will tell you a plain cash register is all you need. Both are right for some sellers and wrong for others. The useful question is not which is better. It is what you need to know at the end of each day.

What a cash register does

A traditional cash register is a machine with a drawer. You key in a price, press a department button, take the money and give change. It prints a receipt, keeps a running total and can print a daily summary.

Strengths:

  • Simple to learn. A new helper can use it in minutes.
  • No phone, no login, no updates.
  • Works in a power cut if it has a battery.

Limits:

  • Records are shallow. Many registers store totals by department, not by item.
  • Adding or changing prices means working through the keypad.
  • Getting data out usually means paper tape or typing numbers into a spreadsheet.
  • Card payments are handled by a separate device.

If you sell a handful of items at round prices, that can be enough.

What a POS app does

A POS app turns a phone or tablet into the register. You tap items from a grid, apply tax and a discount if needed, take payment and the app stores the sale.

Strengths:

  • Itemized records. You can see which products sold.
  • Easy price and product changes.
  • Reports for a day, week or month without adding up paper.
  • Data export to a spreadsheet.

Limits:

  • It depends on a phone or tablet. A dead battery stops sales.
  • Some apps need an account, an internet connection or a monthly fee.
  • A new helper may need a short walkthrough.

The questions that decide it

Skip the feature lists. Answer these instead.

1. Do I need to know what sold, or only how much? If "$640 today" is all you need, a register is fine. If you want to know that the lemon bars outsold the brownies two to one, you need item-level records.

2. How many products do I have? Under 20 and rarely changing, a register works. A rotating menu or growing catalog is easier in an app.

3. Do I charge sales tax on some items and not others? A register can handle this with tax keys, but it takes setup. Apps typically let you mark each product as taxed or not. Check with a tax advisor about which rates apply to what you sell.

4. Will I need records for taxes or bookkeeping? Itemized, exportable records save hours at tax time. A register's paper tape does not.

5. Do I run tabs or hold orders? If a customer orders, walks off and returns, or a table orders in rounds, you need a way to keep open orders. Basic registers do not.

6. Where do I sell, and is there signal? At a market or event with poor coverage, a POS app that needs the internet is a risk. A register is not affected. An app that works fully offline removes that risk.

7. What can I spend monthly? Monthly fees add up. Check the full cost of any app you consider, including fees tied to card processing, export and extra devices. Terms vary by provider, so check each provider's current pricing.

The cost angle

A register is a one-time purchase. A POS app can be free, one-time or a subscription. The price on the front page is not always the full cost, so read what is included. Look at:

  • whether exporting your own data costs extra,
  • whether you need an account to use it,
  • whether it works when the internet is down,
  • and whether features sit behind an upgrade.

You can use a card reader with either option. The reader is its own purchase and has its own fees, so compare those separately.

A middle path: a free offline app

If you want item-level records without a subscription or connection, a free offline app sits between the two. Open POS is one example. It works without internet, with no account, subscription or ads, and it is MIT licensed. In it you can:

  • take cash payments with change calculated from the amount handed over,
  • record card sales taken on your own reader (the reader needs its own connection, and Open POS does not process cards),
  • hold tickets and manage tables,
  • keep a customer list,
  • track expenses,
  • apply a whole-order discount,
  • turn tax on or off per product,
  • see reports for today, this week or this month,
  • and export your sales to CSV.

Open POS is built for a single device. Devices do not sync with each other, so if you need several terminals sharing one catalog, an app built for that is a better fit. The team behind Open POS also makes RebuzzPOS, a separate online POS for businesses that need sync across devices. Open POS exchanges no data with it, and you never need it to use Open POS.

Which should you pick?

Choose a cash register if:

  • you sell a few items at fixed prices,
  • you have one person at the counter,
  • you do not need itemized records,
  • and you keep your own books by hand.

Choose a POS app if:

  • your menu or catalog changes,
  • you want item-level reports and exports,
  • you hold orders or run tables,
  • or you track expenses next to sales.

Use both if you like the drawer and the receipt tape, but also want a digital record. Many sellers keep the drawer and ring sales in an app.

A simple way to test

Run a trial week. Keep your register as usual and ring the same sales in a free app. At the end of the week, compare totals and notice which record answered more questions. If the app showed you something the tape could not, that tells you what you need.

Questions about registers and POS apps

Is a cash register enough for a small shop?

Yes, if you sell a few items, do not need itemized records and keep your own books. It stops being enough when you need item-level sales, reports or exports.

Do I need internet to use a POS app?

Not always. Many apps need a connection or an account, and some include an offline mode that syncs later. Open POS works fully without internet. Check how any app you consider behaves with no signal.

Can I use a POS app and a card reader together?

Yes. Take the card payment on the reader, then record the sale in the app. Each needs to be set up on its own, and the reader needs its own connection.

Are free POS apps really free?

It depends on the app. Some charge for card processing, exports or extra features. Read what is included. Open POS is free, with no subscription, account or ads.

Can I switch later?

Yes. Keep your records exportable. A CSV export lets you move your sales history into a spreadsheet or another tool.
