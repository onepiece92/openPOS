End-of-Day Sales Close for Market Vendors: A Simple Checklist

The market is over, your feet hurt and the truck needs loading. It is tempting to throw the cash box in a bag and deal with it tomorrow. Do the close now instead. It takes 10 to 15 minutes, it catches mistakes while you still remember the day, and it gives you clean records when tax time comes.

Here is a simple routine that works for farmers markets, craft fairs, flea markets and pop-ups.

Before you start: set up the close

Pick a spot out of the crowd, ideally with a flat surface and good light. Have these ready:

  • your starting float amount (written down this morning),
  • your sales record (app, notebook or tape),
  • the card reader's day report, if you take cards,
  • a pen and a notepad or your phone's calculator,
  • an envelope or bag for deposits.

Step 1: Count the cash

  1. Take the cash drawer out of public view.
  2. Sort bills by value, all facing the same way.
  3. Count each pile and write down the total per denomination.
  4. Add coins last.
  5. Write the grand total.

Now subtract your starting float. The result is your cash sales for the day. If the number looks odd, count again before you do anything else.

Then separate the next float. Set aside the amount you will start with next time, and put the rest in your deposit envelope. Counting the float out now saves you from guessing tomorrow morning.

Step 2: Match your totals

You are checking that two or three sources tell the same story.

  • Your sales record. Get the day's total, and if you can, split it by payment type: cash and card.
  • Cash count. Cash sales from Step 1 should equal the cash portion of your record.
  • Card reader report. Its card total should equal the card portion of your record.

If you use an app, open its report for today. In Open POS, for example, the Reports screen shows the sales total, expenses and average order value for today, this week or this month. If you take card payments, you take them on your own reader and record them in the app as paid by card, so you compare the app's card portion with the reader's report.

The matching test is: cash counted minus float, plus card total, equals total sales. If it does, you are done with this step.

If the totals do not match

Small gaps are normal. Work through them in this order:

  1. Recount the cash.
  2. Look for sales you forgot to record, such as a quick sale during a rush.
  3. Look for card payments that failed or are still queued on the reader.
  4. Check for change given incorrectly, or a bill taken from the drawer for a personal purchase.
  5. Write the leftover difference and a guess at the cause. Do not force the books to balance by changing numbers.

Patterns matter more than any single gap. If you are short every Saturday, look at what changes on Saturdays.

Step 3: Record your expenses

Expenses are easy to lose, and they matter, since they reduce what you owe in tax and show whether a market is worth the trip. Record today's costs before you leave:

  • booth or stall fee,
  • ingredients or stock bought for this event,
  • packaging, bags and signs,
  • fuel or parking,
  • card reader fees you already know about,
  • any helper paid in cash.

For each, note the date, what it was, the amount and the payment method. Keep the receipt. A quick photo of it on your phone and the paper in an envelope covers you twice.

In Open POS you can enter expenses next to your sales, so the same screen helps you see revenue and expenses for the day. A spreadsheet or notebook works as well. What matters is doing it the same day.

Step 4: Export or copy your records

A record you cannot get out is risky. Before you pack up:

  • Export your sales for the day or week. In Open POS, sales reports export to CSV, which opens in any spreadsheet (the file covers all sales; filter by date in the spreadsheet).
  • If you use a notebook, take a photo of each page.
  • Save the card reader's day report, either as a screenshot or a download from the provider.
  • Put copies somewhere other than the device you sold on. Save a copy somewhere safe, such as a USB drive or your computer.

Open POS works without internet, and so does its export, so you can do it at the stall or once you are home. The app does not send anything on its own. Because devices do not sync with each other, if two phones took sales, export from both and combine them.

Step 5: Secure the money

  • Put the deposit in a sealed envelope with the date and amount on it.
  • Do not leave it in the vehicle overnight if you can avoid it.
  • Deposit it at the bank as soon as you can, and keep the deposit slip with the day's records.

Step 6: Restock and reset

While the day is fresh, write down what sold out, what did not sell and what you wish you had brought. This is the most valuable note of the day, and it takes two minutes. It tells you what to prepare next time.

Then reset for tomorrow: float set aside, charged phone, reader charged, receipt paper or bags topped up.

The one-page checklist

Copy this onto a card and keep it in the cash box.

  • ☐ Count cash by denomination
  • ☐ Subtract starting float = cash sales
  • ☐ Set aside next float
  • ☐ Get total sales from your record
  • ☐ Compare card total to reader report
  • ☐ Cash + card = total sales?
  • ☐ Note any difference and likely cause
  • ☐ Record all expenses with receipts
  • ☐ Export or photograph your records
  • ☐ Seal and secure the deposit
  • ☐ Note what sold out and what did not

Keeping it quick

The routine gets faster once you make it a habit. Two things help most. First, record each sale as it happens, so you are not reconstructing the day at the end. Second, count the float at the start and write it down, so the end count is one subtraction.

Questions about closing the day

How long should an end-of-day close take?

Usually 10 to 15 minutes once you have a routine. The first few times may take longer.

Do I need to count cash if most of my sales are card?

Yes. Even a small cash drawer should be counted, since small gaps are the easiest to miss and fix early.

What should I keep, and for how long?

Keep sales records, expense receipts and deposit slips. How long depends on your local tax rules, so ask your tax advisor.

Can I use Open POS for this close?

For the records side, yes. Open POS has no cash-count, float or close screen, so you count the drawer by hand. It records cash and card sales, tracks expenses, shows reports for today, this week or this month and exports sales to CSV. It works without internet. Card payments are taken on your own reader, which needs its own connection.

What if I run the stall with a helper on another phone?

Devices do not sync. Export from each phone at close and combine the totals in a spreadsheet.
