Where "free" cash register apps usually charge

Many POS apps are free to download and then charge somewhere else. The common patterns are:

  • a subscription that starts after a free trial;
  • paid exports, where you can enter sales for free but pay to get your own data out;
  • caps on items or sales per day on the free plan;
  • card-processing lock-in, where the app is free because you have to take cards through the vendor.

None of these apply to Open POS. Open POS itself has no paid tier; a separate product, RebuzzPOS, exists for businesses that need cloud sync. Nothing in Open POS is locked behind a payment.

What you get for free

Everything in the app, on every device you install it on. Free, for good, under the MIT license.

Selling

  • Product grid with search, categories and favorites
  • Whole-order discounts (amount or percent)
  • Cash with change, or record a card payment taken on your own card terminal
  • Held tickets: park an order, finish it later

Records

  • Customers
  • Order history and voids
  • Audit log
  • PDF receipts you can save or share, or copy the receipt as text.

Money and data

  • Tax rates, with tax switched on or off per product
  • Expense tracking
  • Reports for today, this week or this month: sales total, expenses, average order value
  • CSV import and export, any time

What it doesn't do

Being free and fully offline comes with trade-offs. It is better to know them before you start.

  • One device, one register. Each phone or tablet keeps its own sales. Nothing syncs between devices, and there are no staff logins.
  • No card processing. Open POS does not take card payments. Take the payment on your own card terminal, then record the sale as paid by card.
  • You keep your own copy. The app never sends your data anywhere, and there is no Open POS cloud copy. Export it to CSV now and then and save it somewhere safe.

If you need several registers in sync, the same team makes RebuzzPOS, a separate online POS. The Support screen in Open POS links to it. Open POS exchanges no data with it, and you never need it to use Open POS.

Privacy you can check

The app's own code makes no network calls: no analytics, no ads, no crash reporting and no cloud services. The source code is public on GitHub. From the next release onwards, the Android build also has no internet permission, so the operating system itself blocks any connection. How to check it.

Questions about the price

Is Open POS really free, with no subscription?

Yes. There is no subscription, no trial, no premium tier, no ads and no in-app purchases. Open POS is open source under the MIT license, and it will stay free.

How do you make money, then?

Not from Open POS. The same team makes RebuzzPOS, a separate online POS with 40+ additional features for businesses that need sync across devices. Open POS exchanges no data with it, and you never need it to use Open POS.

Is there a limit on products or sales?

There is no paid plan, so there is no free-plan cap. The practical limit is the storage on your own phone or tablet.

Can I export my data without paying?

Yes. CSV export is part of the free app, and you can export whenever you like.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. Open POS is on Google Play for Android phones and tablets, and on the App Store for iPhone and iPad. Both are free.

Do I need internet or an account?

No. There is no login and no server. Install it, pick your country and currency, add products and start selling, with or without a signal.
